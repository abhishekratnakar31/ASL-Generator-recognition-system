01 / OVERVIEW & MOTIVATION

Why We Built SignPulse (The Problem Statement)

Over 70 million deaf and hard-of-hearing individuals worldwide rely on sign languages (such as American Sign Language, ASL) as their primary means of daily communication. Despite this, fewer than 1% of the hearing population possesses functional conversational signing skills. This linguistic disconnect produces profound barriers across employment, healthcare, legal systems, education, and digital interfaces.

For decades, computer vision research attempted to solve American Sign Language Recognition (ASLR) by treating it as an off-the-shelf video action classification task, using standard 2D Convolutional Networks (ResNet-50) or 3D Spatiotemporal Networks (I3D, SlowFast). However, when deployed outside clean lab datasets to real-world cameras, these models fail completely:

The Fundamental Vision Trap: Standard full-frame CNNs achieve 80%+ training accuracy by inadvertently memorizing static background cues (wall paint, room lighting, furniture, and clothing patterns). When evaluated on unseen signers in novel environments, accuracy drops to under 12%, while requiring high-end GPUs that cannot run locally on consumer devices.

SignPulse was engineered from first principles to solve this fundamental failure: to replace spurious visual correlation with invariant biomechanical kinematics, delivering robust, signer-independent ASL recognition with sub-35ms latency on commodity CPU and edge hardware.

Figure 1: Visual Baseline Overfitting & Generalization Collapse (Notebook 03). Full-frame RGB features (ImageNet ResNet-50, 2048-D) suffer from severe background memorization. While training loss steadily converges, validation loss on unseen signers diverges rapidly, trapping accuracy at 11.93% Top-1.

04 / MATHEMATICAL FOUNDATIONS

Mathematical Formulations & 349-D Tensor Specification

Raw keypoint coordinates extracted from video frames vary according to the subject's position in the camera frame, body size, and distance from the camera lens. SignPulse applies a rigorous 4-stage mathematical normalization pipeline to transform raw joint detections into an invariant 349-dimensional dense feature tensor:

1. Torso Center & Shoulder Span Normalization (106-D)

The origin is translated to the midpoint of the shoulders, and all coordinates are scaled by the Euclidean distance between left and right shoulders:

$$\mathbf{c}_{\text{shoulder}} = \frac{\mathbf{p}_{\text{left\_shoulder}} + \mathbf{p}_{\text{right\_shoulder}}}{2}$$
$$s_{\text{shoulder}} = \max\left(\|\mathbf{p}_{\text{left\_shoulder}} - \mathbf{p}_{\text{right\_shoulder}}\|_2, \; 16.0\right)$$
$$\hat{\mathbf{p}}_i = \frac{\mathbf{p}_i - \mathbf{c}_{\text{shoulder}}}{s_{\text{shoulder}}} \quad \forall i \in \{1, \dots, 53\}$$

This guarantees that whether a signer sits 1 meter or 4 meters away, or is 5 feet or 6.5 feet tall, their normalized body and hand positions reside in the identical spatial coordinate frame.

2. Wrist-Relative Hand Local Coordinate Anchoring (84-D)

To distinguish fine finger movements (e.g. curling the index finger) from global arm motion (e.g. raising the arm), hand landmarks are anchored directly to the respective wrist joint:

$$\hat{\mathbf{h}}_{j}^{\text{left}} = \frac{\mathbf{h}_j^{\text{left}} - \mathbf{p}_{\text{left\_wrist}}}{s_{\text{hand}}^{\text{left}}}, \quad \hat{\mathbf{h}}_{j}^{\text{right}} = \frac{\mathbf{h}_j^{\text{right}} - \mathbf{p}_{\text{right\_wrist}}}{s_{\text{hand}}^{\text{right}}} \quad \forall j \in \{1, \dots, 21\}$$

3. First-Order Temporal Velocity Differentials (106-D)

Static handshapes alone are ambiguous (e.g., the handshape for "change" vs "appointment"). Temporal kinematics are explicitly encoded via inter-frame displacement vectors:

$$\mathbf{v}_t = \hat{\mathbf{p}}_t - \hat{\mathbf{p}}_{t-1} \quad \forall t \in \{1, \dots, 32\}$$

05 / MODEL EVOLUTION

Model Architectures & Evolutionary Progression

Our research followed a four-generation iterative path, where each generation solved a specific empirical failure diagnosed in the preceding phase:

GEN 1

Visual ResNet-50 Baselines (Notebooks 03 & 04)

Frozen ImageNet ResNet50 (2048-D) + Temporal Transformer. Result: 11.93% Top-1 / 22.94% Top-5. Diagnosed failure: The network exploited background wall color and shirt patterns as shortcuts, causing validation loss to blow up on unseen signers.

GEN 2

53-Keypoint Biomechanical Upgrade (Notebook 04b)

Replaced RGB pixels with 349-D kinematic tensors. Architecture: Linear input projection (349 $\rightarrow$ 192), 2-layer Multi-Head Self-Attention Transformer with 4 heads. Result: 41.28% Top-1 / 68.81% Top-5 (3.7× Leap). Background shortcuts completely eliminated.

GEN 3

Multimodal Vision-Language Alignment (Notebook 04)

Integrated all-MiniLM-L6-v2 sentence transformer text prototypes with scaled cosine contrastive loss (InfoNCE). Result: Video-to-Text Recall@5 reached 70.43% and MRR hit 0.5208. Grounded kinematic sign space into continuous semantic language.

GEN 4 (DEPLOYED)

Multi-Inductive Ensembling & Cosine Head (Notebooks 04c & 05)

Combined 3 structurally diverse inductive biases (Self-Attention Transformer + Recurrent BiLSTM + Multimodal Semantic Projector) with hypersphere cosine classification. Result: 76.52% Top-5 and 83.91% Top-10 on locked unseen test signers.

06 / VISION-LANGUAGE GROUNDING

Multimodal Semantic Grounding with MiniLM-L6-v2

In standard classification, glosses are treated as orthogonal one-hot integers ($y \in \{0, \dots, 99\}$), discarding semantic relationships (e.g. "drink" and "water" share physical semantics, whereas "drink" and "computer" do not). SignPulse bridges sign kinematics and natural language in a shared continuous embedding space:

Contrastive Semantic Prototype Projection

Each vocabulary class is expanded into an explanatory English prompt and encoded into a 384-D text embedding via sentence-transformers/all-MiniLM-L6-v2, then projected onto a 192-D unit hypersphere:

Prompt: "An American Sign Language sign representing the word '{gloss}'"
$$\mathbf{t}_c = \text{Normalize}\left(\mathbf{W}_{\text{proj}} \cdot \text{MiniLM}(\text{Prompt}_c)\right) \in \mathbb{R}^{192}$$

Kinematic video features are projected onto the identical 192-D hypersphere, and trained with a symmetric multi-positive InfoNCE loss function:

$$\mathcal{L}_{\text{InfoNCE}} = -\frac{1}{|P(i)|} \sum_{p \in P(i)} \log \frac{\exp\left(\frac{\mathbf{v}_i \cdot \mathbf{t}_p}{\tau}\right)}{\sum_{j \in \mathcal{B}} \exp\left(\frac{\mathbf{v}_i \cdot \mathbf{t}_j}{\tau}\right)}$$

This regularizes kinematic features against arbitrary class partitions, achieving a remarkable 70.43% Recall@5 and 0.5208 Mean Reciprocal Rank (MRR) in zero-shot cross-modal retrieval.

Figure 4: 2D t-SNE Manifold of Joint Kinematic-Semantic Embedding Space (Notebook 05). Visualizes 349-D kinematic trajectory embeddings projected onto the 192-D hypersphere alongside MiniLM text prototype centroids (MRR: 0.5208, Recall@5: 70.43%). Video gestures naturally coalesce around semantic language concepts.
07 / ENSEMBLING STRATEGY

Multi-Inductive Ensembling (`ensemble_equal_three`)

A single neural architecture inevitably suffers from inductive bias blind spots: Transformers excel at global self-attention across time but struggle with sub-frame phase transitions; BiLSTMs excel at local smooth recurrence but decay over long time horizons. SignPulse ensembles three fundamentally diverse inductive paradigms:

Member 1: Pose Transformer

Inductive Bias: Global Self-Attention

Computes pairwise attention weights between all 32 frames simultaneously. Captures bilateral coordination between both hands regardless of temporal lag.

Top-1: 43.04% | Latency: 11.2ms

Member 2: Pose BiLSTM

Inductive Bias: Sequential Recurrence

Processes time forward and backward sequentially, enforcing smooth acceleration constraints and temporal continuity across gesture execution.

Top-1: 39.45% | Latency: 18.4ms

Member 3: Multimodal Pose

Inductive Bias: Semantic Prototype Alignment

Constrains predictions to natural language semantic clusters via MiniLM text vectors, preventing bizarre category confusions (e.g., animate vs inanimate).

Top-1: 37.83% | Latency: 16.8ms

Equal-Weight Probability Fusion

$$\mathbf{P}_{\text{ensemble}}(c \mid \mathbf{X}) = \frac{1}{3} \mathbf{P}_{\text{Transformer}}(c \mid \mathbf{X}) + \frac{1}{3} \mathbf{P}_{\text{BiLSTM}}(c \mid \mathbf{X}) + \frac{1}{3} \mathbf{P}_{\text{Multimodal}}(c \mid \mathbf{X})$$

Error correlation between the three models is near-zero ($\rho < 0.28$). When one model misclassifies a gesture due to boundary ambiguity, the other two models outvote it, propelling Top-5 accuracy to 76.52% and Top-10 to 83.91%.

Figure 5: Boosted Pose Model & Cosine Classification Head Convergence (Notebook 04c). Normalizing features and weight vectors to unit norm via cosine cross-entropy bounds gradient variance, stabilizes angular margins, and produces consistent multi-head convergence across epochs.

12 / SYNTHESIS & FUTURE HORIZONS

Core Conclusions & Continuous ASL Roadmap

1. Representation Geometry Outweighs Parameter Scale

A 2.1M parameter Pose Transformer trained on normalized 349-D kinematics outperformed a 25.6M parameter ImageNet ResNet-50 by 3.7× in accuracy while executing 12.6× faster. Eliminating background noise at the representation level is orders of magnitude more effective than increasing parameter capacity.

2. Multi-Inductive Ensembling Eliminates Variance

Ensembling recurrent sequence modeling (BiLSTM) with self-attention (Transformer) and semantic language prototypes (MiniLM) drove Top-10 coverage to 83.91% with zero mode collapse (88 active classes), providing a dependable safety net for interactive applications.

3. The Path to Continuous Sign Language Recognition (CSLR)

Building on SignPulse's isolated 100-class foundation, our immediate next milestone is extending the architecture to Continuous Sign Language Recognition (CSLR) using Connectionist Temporal Classification (CTC Loss) and sequence-to-sequence autoregressive decoding, enabling deaf individuals to converse in uninterrupted, continuous sentences.

4. Edge Mobile Deployment

Quantizing our PyTorch models to INT8 ONNX Runtime Mobile / TFLite will allow SignPulse to execute natively on iOS and Android devices without requiring an active internet connection, delivering a truly accessible communication companion for the deaf community.
